# Needs survey and critique (from the Phase I submission, section 4)

Copied from the team's Phase I report so it can be read next to the study survey (`docs/SURVEY_CRITIQUE.md`). Only the layout was
changed.

## Target users and purpose
**Target users:** Deaf and hard-of-hearing adults who sometimes need to communicate with hearing people who do not sign. We also ask
a few hearing people who regularly talk with Deaf customers or colleagues, because they are the readers of the result.

**Purpose:** (1) find out how often and where the communication problem happens, (2) learn what people use today and what is
difficult, (3) check whether they would accept a camera and hand-gesture-only control, and (4) later compare the two techniques on
satisfaction, engagement and usability.

**Practical notes:** the survey is short (about 3 minutes), uses simple words, is available in Arabic and English, has an "I am not
sure" option where it makes sense, is anonymous, and starts with a consent line. We aim to share it through Deaf communities or with
an interpreter's help. Early tests may involve hearing classmates; we will say this clearly as a limit.

## Questions: first draft, problem, final version

| # | First draft | Problem | Final question | Answers | Why we ask it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 Frequency | "Do you often talk to people who do not know sign language?" (Yes / No) | "Often" means different things to different people; yes/no hides the detail | "In a normal month, how many times do you need to communicate with someone who does not know sign language?" | Never / 1 to 3 times / 4 to 10 times / More than 10 times | Is the problem common enough to be worth solving? |
| Q2 Where | "Where is communication hardest for you, at the hospital or in shops?" | Only two places: leading, forces a choice, hides other places | "In which places is it hard to communicate? Choose all that apply." | Clinic or hospital / Shop or market / Government office / Work or university / Home with guests / Other (please write) | Which contexts to build first (the context screen) |
| Q3 Current method | "Do you find writing on your phone slow and annoying?" | Two ideas in one; loaded words; does not ask what else people use | "How easy or difficult is it to communicate by typing on your phone in these places?" + "Which other ways do you use?" | Very easy / Easy / Neutral / Difficult / Very difficult; Gestures / Lip reading / Interpreter / Family or friend / None | What we compete with, and how bad the current situation is |
| Q4 Camera comfort | "Would you use our great new app that uses your camera?" | Leading ("great"); asks about a product they have not seen; no privacy | "The app uses your camera only on your device and does not record video. How comfortable would you be with this?" | Very uncomfortable to Very comfortable (5 points) | Privacy is the biggest risk for acceptance |
| Q5 Hands-only control | "Do you prefer gestures or touch?" | Forces one choice; does not ask how they feel or why | "How would you feel about controlling an app only with hand gestures, with no touching of the screen?" + "What worries you about this, if anything?" | Very negative to Very positive (5 points); short answer | Hands-only input is a hard rule of the project |
| Q6 After the test | "Was the app easy to use?" | Too general; does not say which technique; yes hides differences | For each technique: "It was easy to use", "I felt in control", "I would use this again", "It was enjoyable to use" (agree 1 to 5) and "How many mistakes did the app make?" | Agree 1-5; None / 1 to 2 / 3 or more | The comparison of the two techniques |

## Critique of the whole survey (Phase I)
- **Specific:** each question asks about one thing, one place or one situation, and gives time frames ("in a normal month").
- **Unbiased:** praise words removed, leading choices removed, the same neutral scale used in Q3 to Q6.
- **Balanced:** scales have both positive and negative ends and a neutral middle, and the answer lists include "Other".
- **Limits:** self-reported answers can differ from real behaviour, a small sample will not represent all Deaf users, and sign
  languages differ between countries. The survey is input to the design, not proof. A pilot with two or three people comes first.

**Phase II note:** Q5's follow-up and Q2's "Other (please write)" are open answers. Lab 3 prefers closed questions; they are kept
because they are optional and short, and they are reported as quotes, not scored. Q6 became the study survey in the app; its
critique is in `docs/SURVEY_CRITIQUE.md`.
